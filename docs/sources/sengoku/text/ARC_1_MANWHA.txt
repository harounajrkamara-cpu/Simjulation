

ARC 1 – Li Hattori: The Twin Demon’s Son
1. The Boy Who Shouted in the Square
Winter 1540. Month 10, Day 15. A cold morning in a small mountain village in Shiina territory.
The square is busier than usual—villagers cluster around a notice board, whispering. A mounted officer in dark green and silver—Shiina colors—reads from a parchment:
“By order of Lord Shiina Yasutane, all men aged 15–25 of good health and character are invited to join the military forces of the Shiina Stam. Training begins immediately. Honor and pay await those who serve.”[ppl-ai-file-upload.s3.amazonaws]​
You’re sixteen. Barefoot in halffrozen mud, carrying firewood. Your little sister Kenjiko—five years old—hangs off your sleeve, skipping.
“Big brother, what’s happening? Are you going to fight someone?”
You barely hear her.
Your eyes are locked on that parchment.
You’ve dreamed this moment for years. A nameless warrior with two swords cutting through enemies like a storm, faceless in your dreams but always moving like you want to move—fast, decisive, unstoppable.
You don’t even realize you’re walking forward until you’re no longer in the crowd. People are staring at you.
It doesn’t matter.
You take a breath.
“I’M JOINING THE ARMY!”[ppl-ai-file-upload.s3.amazonaws]​
Kenjiko squeals, “Big brother warrior!” She doesn’t understand what “army” really means. Most of the crowd doesn’t either.
But one man in the square understands perfectly.
A middleaged samurai in modest but wellkept armor, with the calm presence of someone used to command, dismounts and walks straight toward you.
Shiina Yasutane.
Lord of this territory.
For some reason, he says your name like he’s known it for years.
“Li Hattori. You wish to join my forces?”[ppl-ai-file-upload.s3.amazonaws]​
The square goes silent.
You bow, but not too deep, and answer as you are—a loud, sharp boy with fire in his chest.
“Yes, my lord. I’m ready for whatever you throw at me.”
Something flickers in his eyes—recognition, maybe pain. He studies your face like he’s seeing someone else behind it.
“Then speak to your mother first,” he says. “A warrior who honors his family is worth ten who do not. If she permits it, report to the training grounds in three days.”
He lays a hand on your shoulder—strangely protective.
“And train hard, Li. The world is more dangerous than you know. Especially for someone like you.”[ppl-ai-file-upload.s3.amazonaws]​
He leaves you with that.
You don’t yet understand what “someone like you” means.
You will.

2. Three Days Before War
Back home, your mother Yuki is at the loom. Your older sister Mei, fifteen, is hanging laundry outside. Kenjiko clings to your sleeve.
They see your face and already know.
Yuki goes pale.
“No,” she whispers. “No, Li. Don’t say it.”
You sit across from her and force the words out.
“Mother… I saw a recruitment notice today. I want to talk about it.”
Silence stretches.
“You want to join the army,” she says—not asking.
“…Yes.”
Her hands tremble.
“Just like him.”
You freeze.
“Like who?”
“Your father.” Her voice cracks. “You look like him. You sound like him. That fire in your eyes…”[ppl-ai-file-upload.s3.amazonaws]​
She warns you with words that will echo through the rest of your life:
“You don’t understand what you’re asking. The world out there isn’t glory and banners. It’s blood. Betrayal. Death. I kept you hidden for two years, praying every day no one would recognize your face, your name, your—”
She stops herself, choking on memory.
You snap back:
“So what? I stay here forever? Hide like a coward?!”
“You stay alive!” she shouts, tears streaming now. “You stay with your sisters! You stay safe!”
Mei, quiet and steady, finally speaks:
“Mother… he’s going to go anyway. If you forbid it, he’ll just leave in the night. At least this way, we can say goodbye properly.”[ppl-ai-file-upload.s3.amazonaws]​
Yuki looks at you properly for the first time in years and sees that you’re no longer a child.
She breaks.
“Three days,” she whispers. “You have three days before you report?”
“Yes.”
“Then… we have three days.”
She pulls you into a tight embrace.
“Promise me you’ll be careful. Don’t trust anyone too easily. Don’t draw attention to yourself. And don’t—” her voice cracks “—don’t die like he did.”
You promise.
You don’t know yet who killed him. Or why.
But you will.
Day 1 – Secret Training
At dawn, you head into the forest with the two wooden swords you carved yourself to mimic the warrior in your dreams.
You drill alone. Two swords in constant motion. Improvised forms. Footwork. Rhythm.
It feels right. Natural. Like your body remembers something your mind doesn’t.
But Yuki’s warning echoes: “Don’t draw attention like he did.”
If you show up at training with a flashy twosword style, officers will notice. Word could spread. Whoever killed your father might hear of a boy fighting like him and decide to finish the job.
So you make a decision that shapes everything:
Publicly: you fight with one sword.
Privately: you train twosword until your arms shake.
You start living a double life on day one.
Day 2 – Mei
You find Mei behind the house, hanging laundry.
“When I’m gone,” you tell her, “I need you to take care of Mother. And Kenjiko.”
“I know,” she says. She always knew this was coming.
You hand her a small pouch—everything you’ve saved from odd jobs and hunting.
“This is all I have. When I start getting paid—ten koku a year—I’ll send all of it here. Every coin.”[ppl-ai-file-upload.s3.amazonaws]​
She protests. You shut that down.
“I’ll have food and equipment. I don’t need anything else.”
You make her promise not to tell your mother about the money until after you’re gone, because you know Yuki will tell you to keep it.
Mei, eyes wet, tells you something that lodges in your chest:
“Father always called you ‘Little Dragon’ as a baby. He’d say, ‘This one is going to shake the world someday.’”[ppl-ai-file-upload.s3.amazonaws]​
You’ve always felt that call. Now you know he saw it too.
Day 3 – Kenjiko
By the stream, Kenjiko builds a castle from small stones.
“Tomorrow, I’m going away for a while,” you tell her.
“To fight bad guys?” she asks, delighted.
You don’t correct her.
“Like the warriors in the stories,” you say.
“Will you come back and tell me stories?”
“Promise,” you say.
She hugs your neck.
You think: This is what I’m protecting. This innocence. This family.
That night, Yuki gives you a wrapped bundle.
Inside: a simple short sword.
“This was your father’s,” she says. “He wanted you to have it when you were ready. Take it. And come back to give it back to me someday.”
You don’t sleep. You sit under the stars with the sword across your knees, wondering who your father really was.
You don’t know yet that the entire country used to whisper his name.

3. The Forge – Three Months of Hell
Dawn at the Shiina training grounds.
The mountain mist clings to the ground. Thirty recruits stand in a shaky line—boys more than men. Some farmers, some woodcutters, some runaway sons who lied about permission.
At the front: Commander Kaito. Scar across his face. Voice like a whip.
“For the next three months,” he says, “your asses belong to me.”
You’re ready.
“I’m ready for whatever you throw at me, sir!” you shout.
Treacherous words. They become a mirror for all the pain coming.
First Spar
He throws wooden swords to each recruit. You catch yours like it was made for your hand.
You’re paired with Genji, a stocky farm boy who smirks down at you.
“No hard feelings, kid,” he says.
“None taken.”
“BEGIN!”
He charges, swinging like he’s chopping a tree.
You sidestep. Time slows. The opening is obvious.
One strike to his wrist. His sword drops in three seconds.
The yard goes quiet.
“Again,” Kaito says, eyes narrowing.
Second round—Genji more cautious now.
You parry twice, sweep his legs, drop him in five seconds.
Now Kaito’s gaze sharpens.
“Where’d you learn to move like that?”
You don’t flinch.
“Taught myself, sir. Hunting in the mountains. You learn to be quick or you starve.”[ppl-ai-file-upload.s3.amazonaws]​
Halftruth. Your body remembers more than hunting.
Kaito grunts.
“Quick isn’t enough. But it’s a start.”
Basic Training – Month 1
It’s not glamorous.
It’s:
50 pushups in icy mud.
Uphill runs with full packs.
Shield walls under rain that soaks clothes and chills bone.
Learning how to stand in line when your legs shake.
Recruits drop.
From 30, you’re down to 23 by the end of the first month. Seven quit or fail.
You’re always in the top five on runs, sharp in drills, precise with a sword. You deliberately hold back so you’re good—but not suspiciously good.
Kaito’s assessment, overheard one evening:
“Hattori’s got potential. Quick learner. Cocky, but not stupid. Worth watching.”
You also meet Hiro—a lean, nervous boy who’s not good with a sword but has heart. He sits with you in the barracks.
“That was incredible today,” he says. “Where did you really learn to fight?”
You give him the hunting line again and shut it down gently.
But you start thinking: If I get good, I can pull others up with me.
Month 2 – Learning to Kill
The training shifts.
Now it’s not “how to stand in a line.”
It’s:
Where to thrust a spear to get past armor.
How to cut tendons to drop a man fast.
How to move when the ground is slick with blood and rain.
Kaito’s speech sticks with you:
“War isn’t glorious. It’s chaos. Your friends will die. You might die. The only thing that keeps you alive is training, discipline, and never hesitating. When you see an opening—take it. Mercy gets you killed.”[ppl-ai-file-upload.s3.amazonaws]​
Two more recruits drop. 23 → 21.
Secretly Watching the War Behind the War

You hear voices.

From behind a tree, you see them:
Two Takeda officers, red armor muted under cloaks.
And Mitsui Sadamune, one of the five Anegakoji stam leaders—your own clan’s internal nobility.
You listen.
Mitsui:

If Mitsui flips, 18,000 Anegakoji troops will flip with him.

So you do something smarter.

4. The Letter That Changes Everything
Back at the barracks, late.
You take ink and rough paper from the corner.
You write.
“To Lord Shiina Yasutane’s eyes only.
I am no one of importance. But I witnessed Lord Mitsui Sadamune meeting with Takeda officers in secret.
He spoke of opening the Southern Pass. Delaying reinforcements. Asking for a Takeda oath.
I do not know if you already suspect. But I cannot stay silent.
A soldier serves his clan above all.
—A Loyal Shadow”[ppl-ai-file-upload.s3.amazonaws]​
You deliberately write messy so he can’t match handwriting later.
In the dead of night, you slip through shadows to his office and slide the letter under his door.
You have no idea if it will matter.
It does.
Three days later, you’re no longer “just a recruit.”

Kaito studies you, then nods once.
“Smart. Hidden weapons win wars. Surprises turn battles.”

Lord Yasutane Calls
Then Yasutane summons you.
You enter expecting punishment.
He holds up the anonymous letter.
